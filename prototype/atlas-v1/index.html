    Grown-up notes & sources

    Spoken words use your browser’s speech voices. Historical forms use modern voice approximations, not reconstructed historical recordings. Each language keeps a consistent matching speaker. If its voice is still loading, playback waits instead of substituting English. When no Latin voice exists, Latin readings use an explicitly labelled Italian-voice approximation. This is an exploration prototype. Era bands are approximate teaching chapters, not precise dates of origin. The 1700s chapter appears for the piano investigation. Modern words are not used as medieval or Roman translations. Historical labels describe broad areas; borders remain present-day reference outlines. Positions are representative language anchors, not claims that a word originated at one city. Trails show selected linguistic stages, not literal travel routes. Present-day borders help us find places; historical languages crossed those borders. A different word does not by itself disprove a connection: the feedback here uses the selected case’s history.

    Solid trails represent the main borrowing story. Dashed trails show related words on a different branch. “Earlier trail traced” means the selected investigation is complete; words can have a deeper history.

    The Dinosauria stop is modern scientific Latin, not a Roman-era name. Ancient Greece marks the source of the word parts. Evidence descriptions are educational retellings, rather than reproductions of specific documents.
